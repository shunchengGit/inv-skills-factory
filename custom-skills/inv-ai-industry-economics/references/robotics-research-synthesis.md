# Robotics research synthesis: audit checklist

Use when combining a recent industry report with an existing investment knowledge library. This complements the component-profit bridge; it governs synthesis and evidence quality, not stock valuation.

## 1. Build the evidence timeline

- Read the index, search industry terms, component terms and risk/economics terms, follow relevant references, then inspect reverse links and theme tags for missed sources.
- Use the report's publication date, not ingestion or later summary-edit time.
- Label each claim: historical disclosure, third-party statistic, company guidance, broker forecast, demonstration, pilot, paid delivery, repeat purchase, capacity target or MOU.
- Track underlying provenance. Two brokers quoting one prospectus are not two independent financial confirmations; translated editions count as one source.

## 2. Normalize before comparing

Check region, form factor, product line, units, forecast interval and stock-versus-flow definitions. Wheel-based, half-size biped and full-size biped shipment counts imply different component demand. Installed base/cumulative adoption cannot substitute for annual shipments; annual hardware revenue is not cumulative market value or software-inclusive revenue.

When summary numbers conflict, retrieve the relevant original PDF pages and calculate derived metrics with a tool. Example from synthesis: a China shipment projection had been labeled global in an older summary, and its attached CAGR did not match the stated endpoints. Correct the report's interpretation explicitly; do not silently mutate library sources during a read-only research task.

A bibliography title is a retrieval lead, not evidence for the missing report's detailed financial explanation. Search both resource index and filenames; report the scope of a negative search rather than declaring the report nonexistent.

## 3. Test actual production readiness

Ask for complete-task success, autonomous recovery, human interventions, effective operating hours, new-task adaptation time, and cost per completed task. Specify whether human supervision and teleoperation are included.

If illustrating compounded reliability with p**n, state that this assumes independent steps, fixed per-step success and no recovery. It is a hypothetical teaching example, not an empirical estimate of a robot's performance.

Separate demand funded by customer productivity from research, entertainment and data-collection purchases. Both may be real revenue, but they have different repeat-purchase economics and financing dependencies.

## 4. Translate to portfolio research, not automatic trades

- Bottleneck importance does not establish supplier pricing power, stock cheapness or a fixed allocation.
- Map direct versus enabling/narrative exposure using the actual holdings source.
- Check whether quote timestamps represent collection time or actual market sessions; do not call a weekend retrieval a trading-day close without verification.
- Apply the user's current cash rules rather than stale rules embedded in historical notes.
- If current earnings, prices and valuation comparisons are not refreshed, provide research priorities and conditional proof points, not current-price rankings.

## 5. Deliver and verify

Report: conclusion, timeline, normalized evidence, technical/economic bottlenecks, profit-capture risks, institutional disagreements, portfolio implications, measurable triggers, coverage gaps and source index.

Explicitly list original pages checked versus summaries relied on. Verify the saved file and all cited local paths. Separate artifact creation from library ingestion, index rebuild, lint and Git push; claim only operations actually completed.
